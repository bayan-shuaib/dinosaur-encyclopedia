I want to add a new scientific information layer to the existing Dinopedia species pages.

IMPORTANT:
This is NOT a request to redesign the entire species page.

Dinopedia is already an established digital museum / encyclopedia focused primarily on dinosaurs. The current species page structure, visual identity, navigation, layouts, and existing content should be preserved.

The goal is to expand the scientific depth of species pages by introducing a structured way to connect:

Fossil Evidence
→ Observable Traits
→ Scientific Interpretation
→ Confidence / Uncertainty
→ Evolutionary Context
→ Scientific Sources

This should make Dinopedia more scientifically useful while keeping the experience understandable, visual, and museum-like.

Do NOT turn Dinopedia into a university-level scientific database.

Do NOT create a genome database.

Do NOT fabricate dinosaur DNA or genetic sequences.

Do NOT imply that dinosaur genomes are currently known when they are not.

Do NOT add large amounts of complicated scientific terminology just for the sake of appearing advanced.

The system should be understandable to a high-school student and a general museum visitor while still being scientifically responsible.

==================================================
1. WHY THIS FEATURE EXISTS
==================================================

Currently, Dinopedia provides information about things such as:

- appearance
- behavior
- anatomy
- size
- locomotion
- ecology
- comparisons
- scientific information

The problem is that these sections primarily tell the visitor WHAT we think a dinosaur was like.

The new system should additionally explain:

"HOW DO WE KNOW?"

This is important because many dinosaur characteristics are not directly observed. They are reconstructed from fossil evidence, anatomy, comparisons with living animals, biomechanics, trackways, geological evidence, phylogenetic relationships, and other scientific evidence.

The new system should therefore help visitors distinguish between:

1. Directly observed evidence
2. Strong scientific inference
3. Reasonable reconstruction
4. Hypothesis / uncertainty
5. Unknown information

The system should make scientific uncertainty visible rather than hiding it.

==================================================
2. SPECIES PAGE STRUCTURE
==================================================

The current species page already has separate areas such as:

- Life Appearance and Behavior
- Anatomy and Scientific Evidence
- Quick Information
- other existing species information

Preserve the current structure and styling.

I recommend extending the scientific information into the following conceptual sections:

A. LIFE & BEHAVIOR
B. ANATOMY

D. EVOLUTIONARY CONTEXT
E. SCIENTIFIC SOURCES

Do NOT necessarily display all five as huge separate pages.

Use the existing species-page architecture and determine the cleanest way to integrate them.

The important thing is that the visitor can understand the relationship between these categories.

The ideal conceptual flow is:

WHAT WAS IT LIKE?
→ Life & Behavior

WHAT WAS ITS BODY LIKE?
→ Anatomy

HOW DO WE KNOW?
→ Evidence & Traits

HOW DOES IT FIT INTO EVOLUTION?
→ Evolutionary Context

WHERE DID THIS INFORMATION COME FROM?
→ Scientific Sources

3. NEW SECTION: EVIDENCE & TRAITS
==================================================

This is the most important new addition.

Create a new "Evidence & Traits" area within the species experience.

The purpose is to connect individual traits to the evidence supporting them.

Do NOT simply create another block of paragraphs.

Make this structured and visually scannable.

Each trait should ideally have:

TRAIT
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
CONFIDENCE
SOURCE

For example:

--------------------------------
TRAIT
Large, robust skull

EVIDENCE
Preserved cranial fossils and skull morphology

INTERPRETATION
The skull morphology indicates a strongly built feeding apparatus.

CONFIDENCE
High

SOURCE
[scientific citation]
--------------------------------

Another example:

--------------------------------
TRAIT
Possible integumentary covering

EVIDENCE
Evidence from related taxa and phylogenetic context

INTERPRETATION
A covering is scientifically plausible, but the exact appearance is uncertain.

CONFIDENCE
Moderate

SOURCE
[scientific citation]
--------------------------------

The examples above are STRUCTURAL examples only.

Do not invent scientific claims or citations.

The actual content must be based on reliable scientific sources.

==================================================
4. EVIDENCE CATEGORIES
==================================================

Create a simple evidence classification system.

The system should distinguish between several levels of certainty.

Suggested categories:

DIRECT EVIDENCE
Something physically preserved or directly observable in the fossil record.

Examples may include:

- bones
- teeth
- preserved impressions
- trackways
- nests
- eggs
- preserved integument
- pathological features

INFERRED
A scientifically supported conclusion derived from physical evidence.

Examples may include:

- muscle arrangement
- probable locomotion
- feeding mechanics
- body posture

RECONSTRUCTED
A scientifically informed reconstruction where the complete feature is not directly preserved.

Examples may include:

- external appearance
- muscle reconstruction
- soft-tissue arrangement

HYPOTHESIS / UNCERTAIN
A proposed interpretation where significant uncertainty remains.

Examples may include:

- uncertain behavior
- uncertain coloration
- debated ecological interpretation

UNKNOWN
There is currently insufficient evidence to make a reliable claim.

IMPORTANT:

Do not treat these categories as absolute mathematical measurements.

They are communication tools for explaining scientific certainty.

==================================================
5. CONFIDENCE SYSTEM
==================================================

Introduce a simple confidence indicator where useful.

Suggested levels:

HIGH
Evidence is strong and relatively well supported.

MODERATE
Evidence exists, but interpretation contains meaningful uncertainty.

LOW
Evidence is limited or competing interpretations exist.

UNKNOWN
There is insufficient evidence.

Keep this visually simple.

Do NOT create a complicated numerical probability system.

Do NOT assign arbitrary percentages such as "87% confidence" unless such a value is actually supported by the source.

The confidence label should communicate evidence strength, not pretend to calculate scientific certainty.

==================================================
6. IMPORTANT: DISTINGUISH EVIDENCE FROM RECONSTRUCTION
==================================================

This is a major principle of the new system.

Dinopedia should clearly distinguish:

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY PRESERVED

from

WHAT SCIENTISTS RECONSTRUCT

from

WHAT REMAINS UNKNOWN.

For example, a visitor may see an image of a dinosaur with a particular appearance.

The interface should make it clear that:

The image is a scientific reconstruction.

It is NOT a photograph.

It may contain features supported by fossil evidence as well as scientifically informed interpretations.

This principle should also work alongside the existing reconstruction / museum presentation.

Do not make the interface overly warning-heavy.

The goal is clarity, not constant disclaimers.

==================================================
7. NEW SECTION: EVOLUTIONARY CONTEXT
==================================================

Add a lightweight evolutionary context section.

This does NOT need to become a full phylogenetics system.

For each species, where appropriate, provide structured information such as:

- clade
- family
- major evolutionary group
- notable anatomical traits
- evolutionary relationships
- important evolutionary developments

The purpose is to help visitors understand that traits did not appear in isolation.

A trait can be understood in the context of evolutionary relationships.

For example:

Anatomical trait
↓
Related taxa
↓
Earlier / later evolutionary forms
↓
Evolutionary context

Use existing taxonomy where possible.

Do NOT invent evolutionary relationships.

Do NOT create a complicated molecular phylogenetics visualization unless reliable data and a real scientific basis are available.

A simple visual hierarchy or relationship tree is enough for the initial version.

==================================================
8. GENETIC CONTEXT — IMPORTANT
==================================================

Do NOT attempt to add dinosaur genomes.

Do NOT generate fake DNA sequences.

Do NOT imply that complete dinosaur DNA has been recovered.

Instead, introduce a lightweight "Genetic Context" subsection.

This section should explain what is and is not known.

For example, the structure could contain:

GENETIC EVIDENCE

Direct dinosaur genome:
Not currently available

What researchers can study:
- living evolutionary relatives
- comparative genomics
- developmental biology
- evolutionary relationships
- genetic mechanisms in living animals

Why this matters:
Modern genetics can help scientists investigate how traits develop and evolve, even when the original dinosaur genome is unavailable.

IMPORTANT:

These are structural examples.

Do not hardcode scientific claims without verification.

The purpose of this section is to show visitors the difference between:

Fossil evidence
and
Genetic evidence.

If genetic information is unavailable for a species, explicitly saying so is scientifically preferable to inventing data.

20. CONTENT RULES
==================================================

Scientific accuracy is more important than visual complexity.

Do not:

- invent fossil evidence
- invent scientific studies
- invent DNA sequences
- invent genetic information
- present speculation as fact
- assign arbitrary confidence percentages
- claim scientific consensus where there is active disagreement
- create fake citations
- imply that reconstructions are photographs
- imply that every dinosaur trait is genetically known

When scientific uncertainty exists, communicate it clearly.

Use wording such as:

- "Evidence suggests..."
- "Researchers have proposed..."
- "This interpretation is supported by..."
- "The available evidence is limited..."
- "This remains uncertain..."
- "No direct evidence is currently available..."

when scientifically appropriate.

22. IMPLEMENTATION PROCESS
==================================================

Before changing anything:

1. Inspect the existing species-page implementation.
2. Inspect how species data is currently structured.
3. Identify where anatomy, behavior, appearance, and scientific information currently live.
4. Identify the existing citation architecture.
5. Identify the existing species-page tabs / toggles / accordions.
6. Determine the smallest architectural change required.

Then propose the implementation approach.

Do NOT immediately rewrite the species pages.

After implementation:

1. Test existing species pages.
2. Test a species with the new Evidence & Traits information.
3. Test a species without complete evidence information.
4. Confirm missing fields do not break the page.
5. Confirm existing species content still appears.
6. Test desktop.
7. Test mobile.
8. Check responsive layout.
9. Check console errors.
10. Verify that the new system does not create excessive page length or clutter.

==================================================
23. SUCCESS CRITERIA
==================================================

The feature is successful if:

1. Visitors can understand WHAT a dinosaur was like.
2. Visitors can understand HOW scientists know important traits.
3. Visitors can distinguish evidence from reconstruction.
4. Visitors can see uncertainty without being overwhelmed.
5. Species information remains visually approachable.
6. Existing Dinopedia functionality remains intact.
7. The data is structured enough for future scientific citations, Digby, and evidence-update systems.
8. The feature creates a meaningful bridge between paleontology and modern scientific thinking without pretending Dinopedia currently has unavailable genetic data.

==================================================
24. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The central question behind this feature should be:

"How do we know?"

Not:

"How much scientific information can we cram into the page?"

Dinopedia should feel like a museum that doesn't merely tell visitors facts.

It should show them:

WHAT WE KNOW
HOW WE KNOW IT
WHAT WE INFER
WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN
AND WHERE THE EVIDENCE COMES FROM.

Begin by inspecting the existing species-page architecture and data model.

Do not start by rewriting the UI.
